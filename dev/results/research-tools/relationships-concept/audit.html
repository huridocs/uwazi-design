Relationships panel: what each control answers

Five records, captured at 1440×900 in light and dark: the Nepal event “Police fire on protesters at Parliament and New Baneshwar” (216 references), KP Sharma Oli (123), the claim “Hami Nepal organised the 8 September protest” (15), the CEJIL judge Sergio García Ramírez (349) and the Sample judgment Velásquez Rodríguez (9). Frequency is my estimate of a researcher’s use; there is no usage data.

The surface as it opens

1 search with boolean syntax · 2 four view modes · 3 Display menu · 4 Filters · 5 eleven collapsed groups, each a relation-type name and a count · 6 Collapse all / Expand all · 7 the document pane, empty for every Nepal event, Person and Claim (“No document”), 40% of the window.

Expanded: twelve red pills under “led to”. Two point the other way (← “Protesters climb Parliament gate”, ← “Peter 1”), so the group mixes causes and consequences under one label. The type at the right repeats the pill colour.

Tree: the list again, with an evidence count per target. In Nepal every target reads “1”, so the column carries no information.

Graph: “Showing the top 150 of 216 relationships”. Dots have no labels, no status and no date; the picture says that claims and casualty records are numerous, which the group counts already said.

When: the most useful view for an event. It mixes event dates with claim “Asserted on” dates, has no place, and lists “Toll six dead”, “Toll eight dead”, “Toll fourteen dead”, “Toll seventeen dead” as four unrelated rows.

Display: Group by (8 axes), Then by (8), Sort (5), Density (3): 56 grouping pairs and 15 sort-density pairs, before Filters.

Filters: Anchoring and Direction first. No Verification, although every one of the 5,319 Nepal references carries a status; the adapter drops it (data/nepal/profile.ts).

Claim: six “supports” rows and five “disputes” rows, each the article title and its quote. The publisher (Al Jazeera, BBC, Onlinekhabar) is not on the row, so “are the two sides independent?” needs eleven slide-overs.

CEJIL judge: hubs (“HUB · 5 parties”) group the judges who signed one act. The count disagrees across views: Firmantes 174 in the list, 163 in the tree; 349 on the tab, 327 in the graph, 326 on the profile.

Sample judgment: page tags work here and are the panel’s best feature. Case 11.137 appears in three rows across two groups (“Relates to” p.3, p.5; “Mentions” p.9).

Dark: contrast holds; the red pills read heavier than in light and the list becomes a column of red.

Complex for no return

Four ways to draw one list

List, tree and graph show the same rows grouped by relation type. Each has its own counts (174, 163, 327) and its own controls. A researcher learns three views to answer one question.

Arrangement before content

Seven of the toolbar’s controls change how rows are arranged. None says whether a link is confirmed, when it held, or who reported it, though the data has all three.

Direction as a filter

The stored direction is shown as an arrow and offered as a facet, while the question it hides (did this cause the event, or follow it?) is not answered in words.
