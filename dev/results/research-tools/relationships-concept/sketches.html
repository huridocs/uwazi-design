1
One sentence first. Counts, written as a sentence from deriveRelationships and template names. No generated prose; the sentence is a fixed template per collection.
2
Rows are questions, not relation types. A collection maps its relation types to questions once (“led to”, incoming → “What led to it”). Direction becomes wording, so the arrow glyph and the Direction facet go.
3
Strength on the right. The share of confirmed, single-source and disputed references behind the row. Where a collection has no status (CEJIL, Sample) the column shows the evidence count and page tags.
4
Every row scopes References. Clicking a question opens References filtered to it. This replaces the Relationship type facet and Collapse/Expand.
5
Three views, named for what they show. Summary, References, When and where. Tree, Graph, Display and Density are gone.

1
One filter state for strip, map and list. Brushing the strip narrows the map; a map cluster narrows the list. Same atom, three readers.
2
Hours where the data has hours. 15 Nepal events have hour precision; day-precision events sit in a day row, not at midnight.
3
“Not linked” finds gaps. Same place, same hours, no relationship: the journalist shooting at 13:20 is two hops away. A researcher decides whether to link it.
4
Claims leave this view. Their asserted-on dates go to the evidence matrix, so “Toll six/eight/fourteen/seventeen dead” stop filling the strip.

1
A path is a sentence. Each step is a relationship with its own status; the path takes its weakest link’s status.
2
Hubs are named, not hidden. Sources and high-degree organisations connect everything; they are excluded by default and counted.
3
Absence is an answer. When a kind of path does not exist, the tool says so.
